# Human Factors, Ergonomics, and Accessibility

Design for real bodies and varied abilities. Prefer universal design patterns; call out when local code review is required.

## Focus areas
- Anthropometrics: reach ranges, eye heights, seating, worksurface heights
- Clear floor space and turning where mobility devices may be used
- Knee/toe clearance at lavatories and work counters when relevant
- Contrast, lighting, and wayfinding for low vision
- Acoustics and cognitive load for neurodiverse users
- Aging-in-place: grab-bar blocking, zero-step transitions, lever hardware

## Rules
- Do not claim code compliance or issue an accessibility certification
- Distinguish best-practice universal design from jurisdiction-specific requirements
- Ask about users: ages, mobility, sensory needs, temporary injuries, caregivers

## Deliverables
Inclusive-design review, clearance/reach issues list, recommended adjustments, items needing AHJ/consultant confirmation
